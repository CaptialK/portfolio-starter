## The problem

A scheduling tool for clinics offered a fourteen-day free trial. To start one,
you filled in eleven fields: name, work email, password, company, company size,
role, phone, country, how you heard about us, number of locations, and a
checkbox for the newsletter that was ticked by default.

Of the people who opened that form, 34% finished it. Sales had a full pipeline
of leads and a quiet product.

## Constraints

Every field had an owner. Sales needed company size and locations to route
leads. Marketing needed the attribution question. Finance needed country for
tax. The trial itself was one week from a pricing change, so nothing could
happen to billing. And I had no authority to delete anyone's field — only to
argue for it.

## What I did

I found out what each field was actually used for, which meant asking the person
who wanted it and then checking the data behind their answer.

- **Phone** was used by two SDRs, who told me they never called a trial user
  before the trial ended anyway.
- **How did you hear about us** was 61% "Other" or blank. Attribution was
  already coming from the ad platform.
- **Role** had free text in 40% of rows and was not used in any report.
- **Company size** and **number of locations** were both used, but only by the
  routing rule that fired when someone converted — not at signup.

Then I watched eight people sign up, on their own laptops, thinking aloud. Two
abandoned at the phone field. One typed a fake number and said, unprompted,
"they're going to call me."

## Decisions, and why

**Three fields at the door: work email, password, company.** Everything the
product genuinely needed to make an account. Company stayed because the
workspace has to be called something and asking later means an empty state with
a placeholder name in it.

**The rest moved to the first-run screen inside the trial**, where the questions
are no longer a toll and start being setup. Company size and locations became
"how many clinics are you scheduling for?", which is the same data phrased as
something that configures the product. Answer rate on those two went *up*.

**Attribution and phone went away entirely.** I brought the SDRs the number of
calls they'd made to pre-conversion trials in six months — eleven — and they
agreed to drop it. Attribution I traded: I could have the field back the moment
someone showed me a decision that had been made with it. Nobody did.

**The newsletter checkbox got unticked**, which cost us subscribers and was
still right. It was also, I suspect, quietly non-compliant.

## Outcome

Form completion went from 34% to 71%. Completed trials rose 33% month over
month, against a flat top of funnel. Trial-to-paid conversion did not move —
the leads were the same quality, there were just more of them.

The number I did not expect: support tickets about "wrong workspace name" fell
to nearly zero, because people were now naming their workspace while looking at
it instead of during a signup form.

## What I'd change

I ran this as an argument about fields, and it should have been an argument
about where a question belongs. The framing I landed on late — *this question is
a toll at signup and setup inside the product* — would have won every one of
those conversations in a quarter of the time.

And I should have measured earlier. I had the abandonment data on the phone
field a week before I asked for it; I just didn't think to look until two people
walked away from it in front of me.
