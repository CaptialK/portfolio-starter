## The problem

Who it was for and what was broken. One paragraph. Write it so someone who has
never heard of this company understands the stakes by the end of it.

## Constraints

Time, team, tech, and what you weren’t allowed to change. Constraints are
what make the decisions below interesting — without them any choice looks easy.

## What I did

Process and artifacts: sketches, flows, interviews, tests, iterations. Show the
work, not just the result. Images go in `public/work/<slug>/` and get dropped in
as normal Markdown:

![What this screen shows and why it matters](/work/your-slug/step-2.png)

The alt text becomes the caption, so write it as a sentence, not a file name.

## Decisions, and why

The two or three real forks in the road. For each one: what the options were,
what you picked, and what it cost you. **This is the section that gets you
hired.** If you write only one section well, write this one.

> A short quote from a user, a stakeholder, or yourself at the time. Use this
> sparingly — once per case study at most.

## Outcome

Did it ship? Are there numbers? If there are none, say so and say what you
learned instead. An honest "we never shipped it, here's why" reads better than
a vague claim.

## What I’d change

One honest paragraph about what you got wrong or would do differently now.
Reads senior. Nobody believes a case study without one.
