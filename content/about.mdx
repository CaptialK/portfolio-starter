## What I do

Two or three paragraphs, first person, no jargon. What kind of problems you take
on, what you are good at, and what a team gets when they hire you. Write it the
way you would say it to someone across a table — if a sentence would sound
strange out loud, cut it.

Avoid the words "passionate," "innovative," and "user-centric." Everyone claims
them, so they carry no information. Say the specific thing instead: what you
worked on, at what scale, with whom.

## How I work

The habits that are actually yours. Do you start in research, in code, in
sketches? Do you prototype early? Do you write before you draw? This is the part
interviewers quote back at you, so make it true rather than impressive.

## Now

What you're doing at the moment and what you're looking for. Keep this one
short and keep it current — a stale "currently looking for a summer 2024
internship" is worse than no line at all.

## Elsewhere

A short list: writing, side projects, the things you make that aren't client
work. Two or three, with a line each about why they exist.
